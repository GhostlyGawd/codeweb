## Findings

1. **Concrete and user-centred enough for a direction, not an implementation specification.** The primary actor is an agent-heavy developer in their own repository; the decision is reuse or affected-code inspection before changing shared code. Success requires a correct changed decision, rather than a graph or tool invocation. Post-edit and reviewer scenarios support that job. The linked proof sprint supplies ten proposed real changes, native controls, independent adjudication, a provisional decision gate and later voluntary use. These are proposed tests, not completed outcomes. The broader five-row experience table must not become five parallel product commitments.
2. **Capability fit is bounded.** Mapped consumers fit structural queries; reuse candidates fit bounded body similarity and lexical discovery, not semantic understanding of an arbitrary feature request. Similar code need not be merged. Structural deltas fit the documented preserved-baseline/diff loop. Actual behavioral-test status needs evidence from the project's test/CI workflow; Codeweb's static engine cannot supply behavioral correctness. Review evidence can guide source inspection but cannot replace business-invariant/security review. Cross-repo compatibility and automatic host integration are proposals requiring separate proof. The draft explicitly distinguishes proposed behaviour from implemented capability, and preserves these limits.
3. **Conditional does not automatically mean low burden.** Choosing whether an edit is consequential, refreshing maps, reading candidates and resolving stale/empty results all cost effort. The draft correctly includes total inspection/verification effort, false warnings, unnecessary edits and host failures, and allows rejection against native tools. It does not yet define the trigger or output budget. This is acceptable for the strategy because existing output is to be inspected first and the protocol frozen before measurement; it is not ready for a universal automatic hook rollout.
4. **Paid differentiation remains unproved.** Managed checks and source-linked shared evidence sound like ordinary CI until a team demonstrates recurring coordination work those tools leave behind. Hosting is an allowable paid boundary, not evidence of value. The draft explicitly compares checking out both repositories, compiler/tests and normal PR history, and says to yield if those suffice. It requires a recurring job and actual buyer, then payment and renewal. This is an honest commercial hypothesis, not a validated purchase promise.
5. **No smuggled demand, retention or ROI result found.** The proposal labels buying intent and incremental effect unknown, avoids extrapolating public anecdotes to prevalence, separates retained users from organization expansion, and preserves no local telemetry. Price arithmetic is correct and explicitly hypothetical: 10 authors × €10 = €100/month; 10 × 15 minutes × 4 weeks = 10 hours; 10 × assumed €75 = €750 gross time value. All savings/cost inputs are called invented, and setup, triage and maintenance must be subtracted. This does not establish cash savings or that the paid service caused the benefit.
6. **Charter and authority respected.** All local single-repo functionality stays free; paid scope is hosting, multi-repo aggregation or human attention. Accounts/billing do not enter the local product. The charter's price remains intent, not a live offer or a changed price. No participant comparison, outreach, paid pilot, release or charter amendment is authorized by this review. Existing homepage delivery is preserved.

## Weakest load-bearing assumption

A team has a sufficiently frequent cross-repository coordination burden, beyond adequate existing CI, that it will pay and renew for Codeweb's supported service after setup, permissions and triage costs. The underlying research identifies a concrete manual workflow but does not establish recurrence, purchasing authority or willingness to pay. If this fails, the proposed paid path fails even if the free local tool is useful. The central local assumption is also untested: a conditional brief improves real decisions enough to offset its added work.

## Actionable refinements (non-blocking for this proposal)

- In “Make the useful path feel native,” add an explicit provisional trigger example: inspect when the request names a shared implementation or an observed mapped dependency; suppress unsolicited output on small isolated edits; expose an on-demand path and measure mistaken suppression. Freeze trigger, latency/output budget and failure presentation before a participant comparison.
- In the experience table, say “lexical/body-similarity candidates” and identify check statuses as sourced from actual test/CI runs. This makes capability limits visible in the row rather than relying on the qualifying paragraph.
- Next to the paid promise, add one hypothetical before/after coordination example naming who chases which consumer check and which failure the service owns. Mark it hypothetical and require measured net savings attributable to the paid work. Avoid using savings already obtainable from free Codeweb as the justification for the hosted bill.

These refinements strengthen operational clarity; no material false claim requires a corrective attempt. Root owns any author edits and mirroring. This verdict applies only to the hash above.
