# Fifteen bounded assignments

Every lens uses the common full frozen packet and may inspect targeted original sources/capability files. Suggested claim/evidence anchors are starting points, not exclusive evidence or instructions to agree. Refer to frozen `CLAIM-LEDGER.json` and `EVIDENCE.jsonl` for canonical IDs.

## Common finding structure

Each report includes: exact assignment and reviewed-input hash; supported actor/segment/task; strongest supported case; strongest countercase; concrete failure scenario; verdict (`retain`, `narrow`, `revise`, `reject`, `unresolved_from_existing_evidence`); weakest assumption; one small falsification test; and access/coverage limits.

Give material findings stable IDs `L01-F01` etc. For each finding preserve: statement; evidence type; relevant conditions; supporting and opposing evidence IDs with original URLs and passage/capture pointers; implication for the local/paid hypothesis; confidence reason; and unresolved test. Avoid anonymous imagined-customer quotes and unsupported generalization from builders. Keep prose focused; use a structured appendix when nuance cannot fit the main brief.

## 01 — User need and segment

Question: which supported users experience reuse/scope uncertainty during which real tasks, and with what recurrence/consequence? Separate ordinary coding-agent users, tool builders, reviewers, and prospective buyers. Challenge whether the chosen audience is a convenience sample. H1; CW-T018, CW-Z003, CW-X001, CW-Z001 and relevant counterexamples. A frequent complaint about models need not be a structural-tool job. Overturn/narrow if pain is largely rare, builder-specific, or already handled well.

## 02 — Incremental value and alternatives

Question: what useful decision does Codeweb add beyond native grep/LSP/search, compiler/tests, duplication tools, ordinary PR artifacts and CI? H1, H2, H5; CW-X001, CW-L023, CW-Z012, CW-Y005, CW-Y012. Do not equate returned relationships or lower query bytes with changed decisions or lower total cost. Overturn/narrow if the alternatives provide the same actionable answer with less burden.

## 03 — Native experience and attention

Question: does the proposed host flow remove work or create another setup, trigger, refresh, permission and reading job? H3 and host/cold-start/worktree accounts. Challenge the six-line budget, one-second warm target, consumer selection, mistaken suppression, on-demand recovery, and availability assertions. These are proposals, not runtime measurements. Do not perform a fresh host installation experiment under this review scope; identify the smallest needed preflight. Overturn/revise if useful evidence arrives too late, incorrectly, or with excessive interruption.

## 04 — Capability and trust

Question: can current documented Codeweb mechanisms support the promise and expose omissions honestly? H2, H4, H8; CW-Z007, CW-Z011, CW-T022, CW-X013, CW-Z004. Compare mapped consumers, lexical/body similarity, baseline/diff behavior, dynamic/generated gaps and actual test/CI provenance. Distinguish similarity from semantic reuse and static results from behavioral safety. Reject misleading confidence or promises requiring unbuilt capabilities.

## 05 — Repeated reliance

Question: why would the same person return on a later eligible task without prompting, and what does the research actually establish about that? Include infrequent consequential tasks, familiarity, repeat orientation, noise, intentional duplication and successful defaults. Do not infer Codeweb retention from competitor anecdotes, tool installations or a displayed finding. Define repeated-value conditions and the missing direct observation. Narrow if value is predominantly first-time exploration or repeated prompting is required.

## 06 — Buyer and paid-service value

Question: which buyer has a recurring coordination cost the proposed hosted service can actually take over beyond adequate CI/free local work? H6, H7; CW-Y007, CW-Y004, CW-Y001, CW-Y002 and alternatives. Separate users/champions/buyers, hypothetical pricing, gross labor value, net paid-attributable savings, purchase and renewal. Test author-count fit and source-policy/setup costs. Reject/revise the paid hypothesis if it merely repackages free benefits, or the buyer/job is unsupported.

## 07 — Capability expansion

Question: what specific consequential task can a developer undertake more effectively with Codeweb that they would otherwise avoid or need substantial help with? Separate source-backed inspection aid from assumed permission to make larger changes safely. Trace the causal mechanism from evidence to plan/task outcome. A faster lookup may remain a convenience. Identify whether the corpus establishes expansion or only suggests it, and a task that could disprove the claim.

## 08 — Ownership of a complete job

Question: what recurring work is taken over end to end, and what still sits with the user? Map collection, context assembly, freshness, consumer inspection, reuse choice, actual tests, handoffs and result sharing. Include new maintenance/interpretation work introduced by the product. Distinguish a complete factual collection/coordination job from human architecture/business decisions. Revise if the proposed product merely relocates effort or returns a list that leaves the burden intact.

## 09 — Withdrawal test

Question: after familiarity, exactly what recurring loss in time, capability or decision quality would removing Codeweb create? Describe before/with/after-removal work and the strongest substitutes. The public corpus cannot simulate a Codeweb withdrawal result; return a testable mechanism and honest missing evidence. Define matched-task/control/novelty considerations and voluntary reinstatement. Reject/narrow dependence claims based only on liking, reminders, usage volume or artificial lock-in.

## 10 — Compounding project knowledge

Question: can legitimate, trusted project context reduce repeated orientation across changes/sessions and grow in usefulness? Examine mapped facts, versioned evidence, explicit exceptions, freshness, portability, host memory/native substitutes and the proposed saved decisions. Identify what exists versus what is unbuilt. Challenge stale exceptions hiding new problems, metadata maintenance, and unjustified proprietary-data/moat claims. Narrow if accumulation does not remove recurring work.

## 11 — Shared team dependence

Question: do authors, reviewers and repository owners gain from shared checked facts, and what handoffs/conflicting states return if it is removed? Map who needs which source revision, status and decision; inspect ordinary CI/PR history as substitutes. Separate earned shared reliance from a mandated gate or a manager's wish. Identify the supported mechanism and any needed cross-repo/ownership inputs. Reject/revise if coordination still happens manually or the shared artifact adds bureaucracy.

## 12 — Ceiling of the hypothesis

Question: even if the six-line conditional brief works perfectly, is the job substantial enough to support lasting reliance and the desired commercial ambition? Challenge the central proposal, not just its polish. Compare deeper job candidates grounded in the research (context reconstruction, supported change evidence, managed coordination) and their actual capability/buyer limits. The reviewer may recommend retaining a useful free tool while rejecting the proposed paid path, or rebuilding the hypothesis entirely. Do not invent a broad platform as a substitute for evidence.

## 13 — Independent synthesis A

Receive the exact full frozen corpus, twelve completed lens reports and finding tables. Do not receive synthesis B's draft/verdict before your first complete draft is frozen. Record issue/run/session identities and the input packet hash. Reconstruct supported user needs by segment/task/context, identify what the hypotheses explain and fail to explain, and propose the strongest evidence-backed direction. Include the strongest alternative, counterevidence, unresolved conditions and minimal tests. Account for all twelve lenses, preserving source IDs and typed claims. Output `SYNTHESIS-A.md` and its structured claim/finding mapping. You must not approve your own output.

## 14 — Independent synthesis B

Receive identical complete inputs with the same isolation requirement. Independently propose the product/local/paid hypotheses that best explain the evidence and identify their ceiling, reliance mechanisms, alternatives, failure conditions and minimal tests. Both syntheses must consider for/against cases; this is not a forced optimistic/pessimistic pair. Account for all twelve lenses and use stable claim IDs such as `SB-C01`. Freeze first draft before cross-examination. Output `SYNTHESIS-B.md` and its mapping. You must not approve your own output.

## 15 — Adversarial evidence and fidelity review

After both independent drafts are frozen, compare them with all twelve lens reports and the underlying records. Check every material recommendation, quantity/pricing/capability statement, meaningful disagreement and omitted counterexample. Reopen primary sources/capability evidence where needed; record access limitations. Find incorrect inference, collapsed segments, shared errors, local/paid attribution mistakes and omitted conditions. Require explicit dispositions for every material lens finding and usage entries for all original evidence records. Return an actionable correction request or a qualified approval.

The Product Lead then edits the final packet from the reviewed findings, preserving justified disagreements and explaining rejected alternatives. You recheck that exact final artifact against the ledgers, both drafts, twelve reports and frozen hashes. Preserve initial drafts, correction/diff history, and exact reviewed-file hashes. Approval is evidence fidelity and coherent testable recommendations, not proof of customer reliance or demand. This role does not author the synthesis it approves.
