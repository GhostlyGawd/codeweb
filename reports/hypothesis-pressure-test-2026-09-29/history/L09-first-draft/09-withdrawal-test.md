## Findings

### L09-F01 — narrow
Narrow the potential recurring withdrawal loss to rebuilding a useful consumer/reuse inspection shortlist on later consequential changes.

**Supported actor segment task:** Agent-heavy developer changing unfamiliar shared implementations in an owned repository; the strongest reuse account is an experienced solo developer who later built a duplication tool.

**Relevant conditions:** Later tasks introduce changed consumers or unfamiliar implementations; mapping is fresh and covers the relevant source; inspecting the brief costs less than independently reconstructing it.

**Strongest supported case:** CW-Z003 describes repeated human intervention to locate reuse opportunities. Source-backed candidates could shorten that rediscovery before a later edit; removal could return those searches and review prompts. This is an inference, not measured savings.

**Strongest countercase:** CW-X001 reports task-dependent native-search success; CW-Z006 keeps abstraction choice with people. A body match is not semantic suitability and source relationships do not cover all runtime consumers.

**Failure scenario:** A familiar developer already knows the utility, or native search finds the same consumer immediately; the brief adds reading and creates an unnecessary abstraction.

**Weakest assumption:** A later brief changes a correct decision beyond what the now-familiar person and native tools already supply.

**Local implication:** Test repeated net discovery/verification effort and independently judged missed consumers or inappropriate reuse; do not claim safe larger edits or minutes saved yet.

**Paid implication:** Any local gain remains free single-repo value; it supplies no paid-attributable loss.

**Confidence reason:** Moderate confidence in the bounded mechanism and countercases; low confidence in any Codeweb effect because no familiar-user removal comparison exists.

**Minimal falsification test:** After familiarization, compare at least two distinct matched eligible changes with Codeweb against two without, retain normal tests and native tools, and adjudicate inspection/reuse decisions from source. No recurring net advantage falsifies this participant/task mechanism.

**Access coverage limits:** Public accounts are contextual self-reports or small studies, not sampled prevalence or Codeweb users. See evidence index for exact access boundaries.

### L09-F02 — reject
Removal may improve the workflow; voluntary reinstatement must beat capable substitutes, not merely restore a familiar interface.

**Supported actor segment task:** Developers with small, clean or already familiar repositories, and users comparing integration routes.

**Relevant conditions:** Native grep/LSP, source reading, compiler/tests, duplication detection and ordinary host review remain enabled and competently configured in all arms.

**Strongest supported case:** CW-L015 is a specific report of removing Serena on a small repository without seeing benefit; CW-Z012 records verification rereads erasing token savings. These support a credible null or beneficial-removal outcome.

**Strongest countercase:** CW-L024 reports reintroducing some MCP servers after comparing equivalent workflows; that is a useful reinstatement analogy, but concerns browser tooling and a vendor practitioner, not Codeweb dependence.

**Failure scenario:** A control loses its native navigation configuration or gets unfamiliar replacement tools; measured discomfort is then incorrectly attributed to lost Codeweb value.

**Weakest assumption:** Tool preference or invocation volume tracks consequential loss.

**Local implication:** Reject indispensability inferred from liking, reminders, usage counts or installation. Reinstatement plus task-level net benefit is stronger; reinstatement alone remains preference.

**Paid implication:** Paying for an adjacent product or retaining a subscription cannot establish a Codeweb buyer or renewal.

**Confidence reason:** Moderate confidence in the bounded mechanism and countercases; low confidence in any Codeweb effect because no familiar-user removal comparison exists.

**Minimal falsification test:** Keep competent native alternatives available; allow a short adaptation period after removal, then provide a neutral optional re-enable route. Record actual choice and subsequent eligible-task use with reasons and outcomes, not a request to turn it back on.

**Access coverage limits:** Public accounts are contextual self-reports or small studies, not sampled prevalence or Codeweb users. See evidence index for exact access boundaries.

### L09-F03 — narrow
Knowledge and exported evidence that survive removal are a successful durable benefit but weaken claims of ongoing dependence.

**Supported actor segment task:** Developer revisiting a repository or reviewer reusing earlier source-linked facts and decisions.

**Relevant conditions:** Existing notes, source links, Git history and legitimately shared local artifacts stay available; new tasks use changed revisions rather than replaying identical code.

**Strongest supported case:** CW-L037 describes packaging learned workflow quirks into skills to avoid rediscovery. Analogously, learning a project or retaining a useful shortlist can outlast the generating tool.

**Strongest countercase:** CW-L025 replaces integrations with scripts and skills; portable learned procedure can carry the job. Neither account establishes Codeweb project-memory behavior.

**Failure scenario:** The study deletes notes or withholds exported evidence, then counts the imposed reconstruction burden as earned reliance. Alternatively it repeats an identical task and mistakes recall for no value ever delivered.

**Weakest assumption:** Useful new facts must be recomputed frequently enough that retained knowledge cannot replace the tool.

**Local implication:** Distinguish one-time orientation value from fresh-evidence value on later changed source. Do not manufacture lock-in; no claim that proposed saved exceptions are implemented.

**Paid implication:** Exportable history is not a moat or reason to charge for local data; paid loss must involve service work that actually stops.

**Confidence reason:** Moderate confidence in the bounded mechanism and countercases; low confidence in any Codeweb effect because no familiar-user removal comparison exists.

**Minimal falsification test:** Preserve prior artifacts in withdrawal, measure familiar unchanged-code controls and separate new-revision tasks. If benefits survive unaided on later tasks, classify them as learning/portable value, not recurring dependence.

### L09-F05 — revise
The proof sprint can detect first usefulness and voluntary return, but two tasks per participant cannot by itself establish post-familiarity withdrawal loss.

**Supported actor segment task:** Consenting ordinary weekly coding-agent users on real consequential changes; investigators and tool builders must be labeled separately.

**Relevant conditions:** Freeze host/model/package revisions, trigger/output rules, task allocation and scoring before comparison; measure eligible opportunities, not calendar time alone.

**Strongest supported case:** The pilot cautions about small samples and task-dependent interface effects. Verification rereads can cancel narrow savings; the comparison therefore needs total effort and accuracy after familiarization.

**Strongest countercase:** An infrequent consequential task can justify return even with low usage. A short window with no eligible tasks is inconclusive, not rejection; a simple reinstatement can be informative without proving causal magnitude.

**Failure scenario:** An identical task is repeated after the answer is learned; easier tasks land in the removal arm; a new model improves during the trial; a facilitator reminds participants to use the product.

**Weakest assumption:** The proposed observation window supplies enough comparable later tasks after proficiency and novelty have stabilized.

**Local implication:** Add the proposed withdrawal protocol below to a future authorized study; do not relabel the unexecuted ten-change sprint as reliance evidence.

**Paid implication:** Run the paid comparison separately only after a real service/job exists. No participant outreach is authorized by this report.

**Confidence reason:** Moderate confidence in the bounded mechanism and countercases; low confidence in any Codeweb effect because no familiar-user removal comparison exists.

**Minimal falsification test:** Use the preregistered matched-task and continued-access controls below. A single-person alternating test can falsify a strong universal dependence claim but cannot estimate market prevalence.

## Proposed matched withdrawal protocol

**Status:** Proposal only; no trial, recruitment, outreach or product change executed or authorized by this assignment.

**Target estimand:** After familiarization and adaptation, recurring net loss attributable to withdrawing active Codeweb on eligible tasks while keeping learned knowledge, artifacts and strong substitutes.

**Baseline:** Observe each participant normal native workflow before Codeweb: plan, searches, source reading, tests, decisions and elapsed/active effort. Freeze native configuration; do not cripple it.

**Familiarization:** Use distinct consented tasks until the participant can invoke/recover/disable the tool unaided and interpret stale/empty/unsupported outcomes, with at least two later unprompted eligible uses. This proposed criterion is not observed. Log novelty and troubleshooting separately; if proficiency is not reached, classify activation friction, not established withdrawal loss.

**Allocation:** Match distinct tasks prospectively by repository familiarity, mapped scope, expected difficulty, ambiguity, change type and maintainer-estimated risk. Counterbalance active/withdrawn order across participants and task pairs. Use at least two matched pairs per participant after familiarization as a falsification pilot, not a powered prevalence estimate. Include continued-access participants over the same interval to expose learning, calendar and host/model drift.

**Controls:** Include easy/familiar isolated edits and generated/dynamic ambiguous cases; maintain separate eligible-task and control denominators. Do not reuse the exact solved task for time comparisons. Preserve tests and compiler checks in every arm. Record order, interruptions, versions, warm/cold state and access failures.

**Withdrawal:** Disable active Codeweb hooks/tools/instructions and any automatic new brief delivery, verify that state, preserve source, Git history, earlier exports and participant knowledge. For local effect, keep hosted service absent/constant; for a paid test, keep local Codeweb on and pause only incremental service coordination. Allow adaptation before scoring recurring loss; record initial transition cost separately.

**Outcomes:** Per task: active discovery+inspection+verification+triage+refresh/setup amortization minutes; wall-clock wait separately; correct changed inspection/reuse decisions; material omissions; needless reads/edits; completed/failed/skipped/unknown checks. A blinded independent maintainer adjudicates decisions and task difficulty from source and actual project checks; no static result counts as behavioral correctness.

**Decision rule:** Freeze a participant-specific practically meaningful time delta in minutes before measuring. Support a time mechanism only if withdrawn-minus-active net effort exceeds that delta on two distinct eligible pairs without worse active-arm quality. Separately, a consequential omission corrected only with Codeweb can support a decision-quality mechanism after independent adjudication and time accounting; it does not establish population safety. Report mixed and null pairs, not only medians. If substitutes match/better both outcomes after adaptation, reject recurring dependence for that tested segment/task. No eligible tasks, missing checks or access failure leave the withdrawal effect unresolved.

**Reinstatement:** After scored withdrawal, make re-enabling available once in a neutral way with no prompts, rewards, mandated gate or withheld artifacts. Observe whether the participant chooses restoration and uses it on subsequent eligible work. Count opportunities and decisions; record reason and net outcome. Choice alone is not causal evidence; non-return without an eligible opportunity is not rejection.

**Novelty and carryover:** Retained knowledge biases immediate removal toward no loss but is genuine delivered value. Separate unchanged familiar tasks from fresh changes; account for participant learning and continued-access controls. Model stochasticity, unblinded participant expectations and small samples remain limitations even after counterbalancing.

**Ownership and authority:** Product Lead owns proposed sourcing/logistics and any later authorization request; Technical Lead owns host-state validation; a non-author reviewer owns outcome adjudication. Missing customer participation remains missing external evidence, never founder homework or synthetic interviews.
