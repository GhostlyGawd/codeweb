# Lens 10 — Compounding project knowledge

**Verdict: narrow.** Trusted, task-bound structural evidence can preserve what was inspected across sessions. The packet does not establish that accumulating it eliminates recurring orientation, improves with age, or creates paid/proprietary advantage. Saved revision-scoped decisions remain proposed beyond the documented false-positive annotations. Keep the promising local mechanism, test it against maintained native context, and reject the moat inference.

Assignment: can legitimate, trusted project context reduce repeated orientation across changes/sessions and grow in usefulness? Issue COD-89. | Layer | Evidence at the frozen boundary | Limit |

The strongest surviving use is a return to a related consequential change where a prior inspection remains relevant and can be validated cheaply. A familiar isolated edit is a valid case where native search and maintained instructions win. The proposed empirical tests below are not executed work or a build request.

## L10-F01 — Retain a narrow mechanism: task-owned, source-versioned structural receipts can avoid reconstructing which mapped relationships were inspected; accumulating receipts does not establish increasing usefulness or restore task intent.

**Verdict:** narrow. **Evidence types:** repository_observed_capability, inference, unvalidated_customer_effect.

**Actor/task:** An agent-heavy developer returning in a later session to the same supported symbol/change, or handing that inspection to a reviewer.

**Conditions:** Same task and source workspace; retrievable receipt ID; compatible analyzer; supported mapped relationships. Historical source and current source distinguished; current check/reconciliation succeeds. A repeat orientation question actually recurs and the receipt is retrieved before redoing it.

**Strongest supported case:** createReceipt preserves target source hash, graph/inventory/analyzer identity, caller/callee/impact witnesses and unresolved questions. Reconciliation names added, removed and changed witnesses. A later reader can see what was observed without recreating the old pre-edit snapshot. Supporting IDs: K10-RECEIPTS, K10-FRESH.

**Strongest countercase:** The records are explicit task-owned snapshots, not an automatically selected project memory or saved intent/architecture decision system. A persisted graph cannot resolve lost user intent; ordinary Git/PR notes remain a comparator. Opposing IDs: CW-X011, K10-STORE, K10-STRATEGY.

**Failure scenario:** A new session sees an old receipt, assumes its consumer set describes the new change, and skips current inspection; the unknown dynamic consumer never appears in either map.

**Weakest assumption:** Retrieving and validating the relevant old evidence costs less than native reorientation.

**Local implication:** Keep durable evidence free locally; position it as bounded historical inspection support, not self-improving understanding.

**Paid implication:** Persistence alone supplies no paid value beyond local files and existing PR evidence.

**Confidence:** High confidence that primitives exist in frozen source; low confidence in orientation savings because no repeated Codeweb task outcome is present.

**Minimal falsification test:** On one real repository, compare a later eligible change using its prior receipt with a matched change using Git/PR notes plus native search. Include receipt discovery, reconciliation, reading and verification time. Reject the compounding mechanism if no previously completed orientation step is eliminated or any stale inference changes the plan incorrectly. Proposed only; neither a host experiment nor a participant trial executed.

## L10-F02 — Existing false-positive suppression is not proof of revision-safe saved decisions; a rationale must become eligible for rechecking when its relevant assumptions change.

**Verdict:** revise. **Evidence types:** documented_capability, proposal, risk_inference.

**Actor/task:** A developer repeatedly reviewing intentional duplication or a suspected false-positive dead-code finding.

**Conditions:** Decision is deliberate and attributable, with rationale and applicable scope. Relevant source/relationship/ownership changes invalidate or visibly reopen it. Suppressed counts and on-demand inspection remain visible.

**Strongest supported case:** The frozen interface documents codeweb_annotate with a finding fingerprint, optional note and list mode; the documentation describes sidecar suppressions and separate suppressed counts. Intentional duplication in CW-Z006 gives a legitimate reason to remember an exception. Supporting IDs: K10-ANNOTATE, CW-Z006.

**Strongest countercase:** The proposal explicitly calls remembered duplication/scope decisions future conveniences. Identity-based fingerprints are documented, but the frozen packet lacks their implementation and proof that unchanged identity with changed semantics reopens a decision. Opposing IDs: K10-STRATEGY, K10-FRESH.

**Failure scenario:** Two intentionally separate implementations retain their identities while one gains an authorization constraint; an old ignore decision prevents attention to newly consequential divergence. Conversely, invalidating on every unrelated edit recreates all the old triage.

**Weakest assumption:** A small, stable set of revision dependencies can distinguish still-valid rationale from obsolete rationale without excessive user maintenance.

**Local implication:** Treat existing annotation notes separately from proposed revision-scoped decisions. Proposed minimum record: target/finding identity, rationale, source revision or hashes, author, scope and recheck trigger; rechecking must be explicit rather than silently clearing evidence.

**Paid implication:** Shared exception governance could be service work only if it removes demonstrated recurring coordination; an exception count is not evidence of value.

**Confidence:** The gap between interface/documentation and promised semantics is clear. This is a verification requirement, not a reproduced product bug.

**Minimal falsification test:** Using a legitimate suppression, compare an irrelevant source edit, a relevant body/consumer change with stable target identity, and a rename. Require the benign case to remain inspectable without repeat adjudication and relevant changes to request recheck. Fail if a material new risk is hidden or all changes force full re-triage. No suppression behavior experiment executed; annotation invalidation internals were not frozen.

## L10-F03 — Reusable knowledge has credible native and user-owned substitutes; Codeweb must add maintained source evidence rather than claim ownership of project memory.

**Verdict:** narrow. **Evidence types:** public_self_report, maintainer_statement, comparative_inference.

**Actor/task:** A developer who already maintains skills, repository instructions or review guidelines and wants later sessions to avoid relearning procedures.

**Conditions:** Host loads the intended material in the actual session. Instructions are kept current and source-linked. Comparison preserves the user's familiar memory workflow rather than artificially removing it.

**Strongest supported case:** CW-L037 describes turning discovered connector quirks into reusable skills; CW-L034 describes a user-owned runbook store used across hosts. They make saved local context a plausible source of repeat value. Supporting IDs: CW-L037, CW-L034.

**Strongest countercase:** Both are builder accounts and demonstrate alternatives, not Codeweb dependence. CW-Y008 records a historical rules-reference failure followed by maintainer-reported support, so that old gap cannot justify a new permanent memory layer. Opposing IDs: CW-Y008, K10-STRATEGY.

**Failure scenario:** The brief repeats a fact already present in maintained repository instructions; the user now reconciles two conflicting copies and reads more context per task.

**Weakest assumption:** Source-derived changes provide incremental value after native memory and maintained instructions are available.

**Local implication:** Complement user-owned instructions with inspectable structural facts and changes. Do not rebuild a generic memory system from these reports.

**Paid implication:** A buyer can already share instructions and PR history; demonstrate removed coordination beyond that baseline before attributing value to hosting.

**Confidence:** Moderate support for reusable-context practice, weak transfer to ordinary Codeweb users; no measured substitute comparison.

**Minimal falsification test:** For a later task, hold ordinary host memory, skills, Git history and instructions constant in both conditions. Add only the Codeweb evidence delta. If the same correct decision is reached with equal or less total work in control, drop the additional-memory claim for that task class. Current automatic-memory feature parity across hosts is not established; no version-specific host capability claim is made.

## L10-F04 — Knowledge compounds only when avoided reconstruction exceeds capture, freshness, retrieval and exception-maintenance work; storage growth is not an outcome.

**Verdict:** narrow. **Evidence types:** repository_observed_capability, public_self_report, economic_inference.

**Actor/task:** A repository maintainer returning to related changes over time and responsible for keeping contextual metadata trustworthy.

**Conditions:** Old evidence remains locatable and relevant. Maintenance, source migration and false warnings are counted. Familiar isolated changes are valid no-benefit controls.

**Strongest supported case:** Content capture and reconciliation can mechanize part of freshness checking. CW-Z006 describes CI validation of instruction facts, while CW-L034's metadata/review-date practice illustrates explicit context maintenance. Supporting IDs: K10-FRESH, CW-Z006, CW-L034.

**Strongest countercase:** Existing storage has 2 MiB per-record and 32 MiB total limits with no automatic cleanup; target renames require recapture. Builder-reported instruction systems do not show that their maintenance is cheaper than repeated orientation. CW-T012 records stale guidance at a particular commit. Opposing IDs: K10-STORE, CW-T012.

**Failure scenario:** After several refactors, obsolete receipts fill the store and renamed targets cannot reconcile. The maintainer curates records, repairs paths and rereads source for each task, consuming all claimed savings.

**Weakest assumption:** The ratio of genuinely reusable facts to obsolete or redundant facts improves over successive tasks.

**Local implication:** Measure net work removed, not number of facts/receipts. Preserve historical baselines deliberately; recapture must not overwrite the pre-edit evidence needed for verification.

**Paid implication:** Subtract onboarding, metadata ownership, triage and retention/deletion work from any service saving; managed retention is not yet a demonstrated paid job.

**Confidence:** Maintenance requirements are directly visible in code/docs; net customer benefit remains unmeasured.

**Minimal falsification test:** Track three related changes across fresh sessions with a maintainer: record minutes of orientation avoided minus capture, retrieval, freshness checking, curation and correction. Include a rename and an irrelevant edit. Reject 'compounding' if cumulative net savings do not become positive or if accuracy declines. Three changes are a small falsification screen, not a retention estimate; no measurements invented.

## L10-F05 — Portable inspectable records and legitimate information boundaries are prerequisites for shared context; current task/workspace binding is not seamless cross-project memory.

**Verdict:** revise. **Evidence types:** repository_observed_capability, public_requirement, design_inference.

**Actor/task:** A developer moving workspaces/hosts or a reviewer accessing historical evidence, and an organization evaluator with repository isolation rules.

**Conditions:** Source scope and allowed readers remain attached to derived facts. Export preserves source/analyzer identity and unknowns. Historical inspection is distinguished from trusted import/reconciliation in another checkout.

**Strongest supported case:** JSON records, immutable paging and hashes support inspectability; explicit task/root/parent validation guards against accidental mixing. CW-Y004 shows a real evaluator asking about provenance and non-transitive derived-context boundaries. Supporting IDs: K10-STORE, CW-Y004.

**Strongest countercase:** readRecord checks real source-root equality, task and graph-relative path. Copying JSON is therefore not enough to establish working relocation/import. Local execution alone also does not establish what a connected model may receive. Opposing IDs: K10-STORE, CW-L034.

**Failure scenario:** A copied receipt either fails in a new checkout or is manually stripped of identity checks to make it load; later, a shared summary carries facts from repository A into forbidden repository C through allowed intermediary B.

**Weakest assumption:** Portable handoff can preserve provenance and permissions without creating expensive remapping and policy-maintenance work.

**Local implication:** Describe present records as inspectable local artifacts with workspace-bound operational reuse. Any future export/import must preserve constraints rather than disabling the guards.

**Paid implication:** Do not sell pooled learning across repos without scoped provenance, deletion and allowed-flow semantics. CW-Y004 is a requirement signal, not proof of breach or buying authority.

**Confidence:** Workspace checks are explicit in inspected source; cross-host runtime portability and organizational controls remain untested/unbuilt in reviewed materials.

**Minimal falsification test:** Copy a receipt and its source revision to another permitted checkout and inspect it without losing identity; separately test attempted reconciliation under a wrong task/root and an unauthorized A-to-C derived-context transfer. Fail portability claims if a safe handoff requires bypassing guards; fail legitimate accumulation if disallowed facts leak. Proposed boundary test only; no relocation, service or authorization experiment executed.

## L10-F06 — Reject proprietary-data or moat claims from evidence accumulation; narrow the paid hypothesis to demonstrably removed service coordination.

**Verdict:** reject. **Evidence types:** ratified_boundary, proposal, unsupported_commercial_inference.

**Actor/task:** A potential team buyer already coordinating supported checks and source-linked handoffs across explicitly related repositories.

**Conditions:** Local project facts and decisions remain free and user-controlled. Paid service savings are isolated from free local and ordinary CI savings. Payment and renewal require actual buyer evidence.

**Strongest supported case:** The strategy proposes a concrete service job: coordinate agreed checks, rerun on relevant changes and expose tested revisions and unresolved states. Shared source evidence could reduce repeated handoff assembly. Supporting IDs: K10-STRATEGY, K10-RECEIPTS.

**Strongest countercase:** The strategy itself says history alone is not a moat or purchase; the charter keeps laptop/single-repo work free. Skills, Git/PR artifacts and native shared rules supply alternatives. No Codeweb buyer, renewal or exclusive data advantage is established. Opposing IDs: K10-CHARTER, CW-Y008, CW-Y004.

**Failure scenario:** A team accumulates many records but still chases owners, reruns CI and explains failures manually; removing the paid view loses a dashboard but no recurring work.

**Weakest assumption:** Hosting the evidence removes a recurring coordination step that adequate CI and local records do not already remove.

**Local implication:** Earn reliance through trustworthy evidence, not artificial data lock-in. A useful free local tool can survive a rejected moat claim.

**Paid implication:** Retain only a separately testable managed-coordination hypothesis; do not infer purchase, renewal or proprietary advantage from project history.

**Confidence:** Strong grounds to reject the asserted commercial inference; low confidence about whether a narrower future service will succeed.

**Minimal falsification test:** On one consenting team's real recurring cross-repo check, inventory the actual chasing, rerunning and evidence-assembly steps with existing CI/PR practice; compare a bounded coordination prototype including setup and triage. If it removes no step or has nonpositive net savings, reject that paid job. Purchase and renewal would still need separate observation. No buyer trial, external send, paid offer or service build authorized or executed in this assignment.
