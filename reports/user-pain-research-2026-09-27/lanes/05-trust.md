# Lane 5 — Team review and trust

Final bounded collection handoff for COD-60; independent review pending. Initial and supplement evidence IDs resolve through the two L456 JSONL corpora. These are public secondary accounts, not interviews or measured Codeweb outcomes. Counts and access limits: L456-HANDOFF.md.

1. Structural review is a plausible job, with contrary evidence. dafelst reports duplicates/coupling missed in large noisy PRs (CW-L048); joshgachnang reports a custom Claude architecture reviewer already finding those problems (CW-L051). The latter directly challenges the need for another tool.
2. OSS review also preserves accountability and mentoring. Godot and Jamie Tanna describe maintainer pressure (CW-L039, CW-L030). A caller map cannot replace responsible contributors or human communication.
3. Filtering can harm newcomers. Kubernetes participants report both CLA/disclosure controls and mistaken bot accusations (CW-L031–032). Do not equate all AI-assisted contributions with unacceptable contributions.
4. Existing host review and tests remain substantive substitutes (CW-L049–050, CW-L001). Reports of repeated suggestions coexist with useful bounded checks and approval outcomes (CW-L003–005).
5. More context is not a monotonic quality improvement. The small firmware study (CW-L023) reports fewer recurring false positives with repository access and lower recall. Keep author adjudication and limited-task scope visible.
6. Centralized review is a different adoption job from voluntary local checks (CW-L021–022). Cloudflare reports building a configurable internal system (CW-L056), but it is vendor self-use and a partial article read, not a verified general result.
7. Private-source constraints are poorly established. CW-L040 is a services vendor selling local hosting; CW-L034 is a personal builder. Neither proves ordinary enterprise procurement demand. Do not infer that hosted Teams is acceptable from local-product privacy.

Segments and moments: maintainers allocating scarce review attention; team developers orienting in large PRs; reviewers checking architectural intent. Fit inference: deterministic caller/impact/duplicate evidence may help orientation, while business intent, runtime behavior, policy and authorship require other evidence.

Audit change: a receipt must make a claim adjudicable, not turn a pass badge into trust. Include evidence locations, scope, unresolved impacts, actual checks and limitations. Preserve human decision context; do not claim a map solves the review queue.

Proposed experiment: compare reviewers using native tools versus a concise structural brief on a cross-file PR, with a known dynamic dependency and an intentionally sufficient native-check case. Measure time, false findings and missed impacts. Not executed.

Remaining decision-sensitive gaps: no adjudicated reviewer study leaves incremental benefit unknown; private-source purchasing constraints may exclude hosted delivery; OSS willingness to fund human attention is unproven even where burden is severe.
