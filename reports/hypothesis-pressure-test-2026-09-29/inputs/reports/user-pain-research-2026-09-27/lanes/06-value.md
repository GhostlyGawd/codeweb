6. Internal tools compete with buying (CW-L020, CW-L056), and native subscriptions compete with review add-ons (CW-L008). No source establishes willingness to pay for Codeweb.
7. Existing renewal concern records remain concern, not completed churn (CW-L010–013). Keep the original withdrawn extra-charge allegation withdrawn.

Segments and moments: personal subscribers choosing tools, team users experiencing budget changes, and maintainers considering shared checks. Procurement authority is mostly unknown. Current offers and prices are not verified by these historical accounts.

Audit change: paid value needs a distinct recurring shared job and predictable service behavior. Additional local analysis is not a paid wedge: the charter keeps every single-repo local feature free. Hosting, multi-repo aggregation and human attention are permissible hypotheses, not validated demand.

Proposed experiment: after synthesis and separate outreach authorization, test a concrete shared-review service offer against existing CI/internal-wrapper alternatives. Specify scope and predictable usage, and require a real buying decision rather than stated interest. This is a proposal; no offer sent or money collected.

Remaining decision-sensitive gaps: completed team renewals outside selected tools could change the commercial priority; actual buyer authority could reveal user/buyer mismatch; procurement data-egress rules could rule out hosted Teams; unknown Codeweb incremental benefit prevents defensible pricing or purchase prediction.
